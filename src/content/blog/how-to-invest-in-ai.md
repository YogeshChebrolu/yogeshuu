Don't just invest in a single AI company and say you have invested in AI. There are many layers to it from memory to chip manufacturing to GPUs to the cloud to AI research labs, each dominated by different companies, operating at different margins, with different risks.

This is my attempt at building a portfolio that covers the whole stack. It's not straightforward because companies at each layer have different valuations, profit margins, competitive landscapes, and geo-political risks. But once you see the structure, it becomes clear where the real opportunity is.

## Understanding AI supply chain:

Think of AI as a 5-layer stack. The further down you go, the higher the profit margins. It can be seen as 5 layers:

![The AI supply chain as a 5-layer stack](/blog/ai/ai-stack.jpg)

- **AI Research Labs:** OpenAI, Anthropic, DeepMind and xAI. These companies do world-class research, magic maths and build intelligence then sell it as tokens. They need to invest billions in infrastructure and compute before making money and face insane competition from each other.
- **AI Accelerators (GPUs):** Nvidia controls over 90% of the AI accelerator market. Their entire output is booked out through 2028.
- **Cloud:** AWS, GCP, and Azure sit above the hardware. They buy the chips, build the datacentres, and sell compute to everyone else. Massive market, but also massive capex for cooling, memory, power infrastructure all eat into margins.
- **Chip Manufacturing:** TSMC holds an overwhelming share of advanced chip fabrication at 2nm, 3nm, and 4nm. Clients include Nvidia and Apple.
- **Memory:** SK Hynix and Samsung together control over 70% of the entire High Bandwidth Memory market. Every AI model running anywhere in the world touches their chips.

A perfect AI portfolio would include all the companies in the AI stack. So, when your friend says they've invested in AI by buying Nvidia, they've covered only one layer out of five.

## Volatility vs Returns vs Valuation:

- Companies at the top of the stack (OpenAI, Anthropic) come with high volatility, sky high valuations they need to constantly raise capital and maintain competitive advantage over each other.
- But companies at the bottom of the stack (SK Hynix, Micron, Samsung) sit upon decades of research. Hard for someone to disrupt. Most of them have a monopoly or duopoly in their market. It's a reason why they are able to demand very high margins >70%.

Now, let's compare returns and valuations of market leaders at each layer.

![Returns and valuations of market leaders at each layer](/blog/ai/returns.jpg)

- Even though SK Hynix has grown 10x in the last 1 year it's still undervalued at 7.5x forward PE. It's a no brainer to include this company in our portfolio.
- SpaceX, Anthropic, and OpenAI are expected to IPO this year. The valuation at listing will leave little on the table for retail investors.

We need to account for this when designing our portfolio.

## My Thesis:

- It doesn't matter whether OpenAI or Anthropic wins. Both depend on hyperscalers for compute, Nvidia for GPUs, and SK Hynix for HBM. The bottom of the stack wins regardless of who comes out on top which is exactly why I allocate more weight there.
- We are entering an economy where growth no longer depends on labour hours or human productivity but on the number of tokens generated. Every company in this stack is taking a share of that economy.

## Portfolio Allocation:

We can divide our AI portfolio into just 3 parts: **Memory**, **Chip Manufacturing**, and the **AI Application** Layer.

- Memory is dominated by South Korea, Chip Manufacturing by Taiwan. Buying country level ETFs like **EWY** and **EWT** gets us there but we end up holding Korean banks, car manufacturers, and consumer electronics which has nothing to do with AI.
- **DRAM** and **CHAT** ETFs solve this. DRAM invests purely in memory stocks. CHAT covers broad companies across memory, cloud, AI research and application layer companies.

![DRAM and CHAT ETF holdings](/blog/ai/etfs.jpg)

- For chip manufacturing, you can include TSMC directly via its ADR listed on Nasdaq.
- Nasdaq ETF (QQQ) gives broader Nasdaq 100 exposure and captures upcoming IPOs like SpaceX, OpenAI, and Anthropic when they list.

My allocation strategy would be 40% DRAM, 35% TSMC ADR and 25% CHAT ETF.

![Portfolio allocation: 40% DRAM, 35% TSMC ADR, 25% CHAT ETF](/blog/ai/allocation.jpg)

## Risks:

- **Geo-political tensions:** Memory stocks are dominated by South Korea, TSMC from Taiwan and any border tensions there would have a negative impact.
- **Slow in AI adoption:** Which I don't think is happening or will happen but a significant risk to be noted.

## Final Thoughts:
